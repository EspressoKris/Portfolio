## Key findings

- *TP53* mutations are rare in CMML (about 1–2% of patients) but mark poor outcomes
- About 15% of *TP53* wild-type CMMLs have abnormally low *TP53* expression
- Low *TP53* expression predicts poorer response to hypomethylating agents and shorter survival, independent of CPSS and CPSS-Mol risk scores
- Low-*TP53* cells show stem-cell, quiescence and inflammatory signatures
- Azacitidine combined with a dual MDMX/MDM2 inhibitor is synergistic in primary patient cells ex vivo

## Summary

*TP53* mutations are common across myeloid cancers but rare in chronic myelomonocytic leukaemia (CMML), confirmed here in 648 UK and 92 Taiwanese patients. Looking beyond mutations, we found that about 15% of *TP53* wild-type patients have abnormally low *TP53* expression in their bone marrow cells. These patients responded less well to hypomethylating agents and had shorter leukaemia-free and overall survival, independently of existing risk scores; low p53 protein by immunohistochemistry was also linked to worse survival. Low-*TP53* cells showed stem-cell and quiescence programmes, depleted p53-dependent pathways and heightened inflammation, distinct from the biology of *TP53*-mutant cancers. Combining azacitidine with NSC-207895, a dual MDMX/MDM2 inhibitor that activates p53, was strongly synergistic in primary samples from 10 of 11 patients ex vivo, suggesting a way to restore sensitivity to hypomethylating agents.
